# How long does it take ChatGPT to cite me after I fix my website?

Nobody can give you an exact date. OpenAI does not publish how long ChatGPT takes to use a new page, and whether it cites you depends on the question and on who else answers it. What you can have is a schedule to measure it: Peaje checks the page the day you publish it and measures again at 2 and 6 weeks. At 2 weeks you see whether the assistants found it. At 6, whether it stayed among their sources.

Updated: November 2026

The only timing figure OpenAI gives about its search is about 24 hours, and it refers to how long its systems take to adjust after you change your robots.txt, according to its [bots page](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots). For new pages it gives no timeframe.

## Why can nobody give you a date?

Because there are two steps and only one depends on you. The first is that the assistant reads your site again and understands what is new. You fix that one: the bots can get in, the page loads and it answers the question in the first paragraph. The second is that it picks you as a source when someone asks. The model decides that, and it changes with how the question is worded, the day and the other pages it finds.

OpenAI is clear about one thing on its bots page: if you block OAI-SearchBot, your site does not show up in ChatGPT search answers. Letting it in is the requirement for being cited, and even then there is no date. How to let it in is in [which AI bots visit my website](https://usepeaje.com/blog/which-ai-bots-visit-my-website-and-how-do-i-let-them-in).

## How do I check myself whether ChatGPT cites me yet?

Ask the way a customer would, in a new conversation with search turned on. Copy these and change what is in brackets:

- "What options are there for [what you sell] in [your country]?"
- "Recommend three [your category] providers and tell me what each one charges."
- "What does [yourdomain.com] sell and at what price?"

The first two do not include your brand, so they measure whether it brings you up on its own. The third measures whether it knows who you are. If the third one fails, ChatGPT still does not understand what you sell or what you charge, and that is the place to start. Run the round on the same day every week: one answer is a snapshot, and the trend is what matters. We explain it in [how often to check whether ChatGPT and Claude cite you](https://usepeaje.com/blog/how-often-should-i-check-whether-chatgpt-and-claude-cite-my-business).

## What if I still do not show up at 6 weeks?

Look at what the measurement says before changing everything:

- **The bots visited the page and do not cite you.** Content is usually missing: more questions answered with concrete facts from your site, like prices, timelines and terms.
- **The bots did not visit it.** Something is blocking it. Check your robots.txt, your firewall or your CDN, and that the page is in your sitemap.
- **The fix stopped working.** A later change broke it. The measurement flags it, and the daily monitor leaves you a task when something on your site fails.

Your agent tells you which of the three it is and leaves the next task ready. If you are already indexed and still not named, see [why ChatGPT does not mention you](https://usepeaje.com/blog/my-website-works-but-chatgpt-does-not-mention-me-why).

Telling your agent or giving it the URL is enough. It marks the task done, Peaje checks the page on your live site and, if it passes, schedules the measurements at 2 and 6 weeks.
