# Do I need my website in English and Spanish for AI to cite me?

Only if you have customers who ask in English. ChatGPT and Claude search the web starting from your customer's question, so if your customers ask in Spanish, a good Spanish page is the one that counts. If you also sell to people who ask in English, give each language its own page, with all the content translated, and measure the same questions in both languages to see where you get cited.

## How does the assistant decide what language to search in and whom to cite?

Neither OpenAI's help pages nor Anthropic's documentation explain how they pick the language. Here is what is written down:

- **ChatGPT.** According to [OpenAI's help article on its search](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-chatgpt-search), ChatGPT usually rewrites the question into one or more targeted searches and sends them to its search providers. It can also use an approximate location from your IP address to give local results. The article says nothing about the language of those searches.
- **Claude.** [Anthropic's documentation](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/web-search-tool) says Claude decides when to search based on the question, can search several times within one answer and returns the sources it cited.

For your business, this means the search starts from the customer's question, and in ChatGPT where they ask from can count too. You cannot control it, but you can measure it. Ask the same question in Spanish and in English and look at which sources come up in each.

## When does it pay to have pages in two languages?

| Your case | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| You sell in your city or country and your customers write in Spanish | One site in Spanish, done well. Translating it does not bring you closer to those customers |
| You sell abroad, to tourists or to companies in other countries | Both versions of your main pages: what you sell, pricing, terms and FAQ |
| You sell software or services and your customers search in both languages | Both versions, and measure where you get cited before translating everything |
| You do not know what language they ask in | Check your inbox and your sales chat. The language of those questions is your answer |

If you are going to translate, start with the pages that answer buying questions. A whole blog in English does not help if your pricing page is still only in Spanish.

## How do I build both versions without mixing them?

The rules used here are Google's, from its [guide to multi-regional and multilingual sites](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites). They are written for its search engine and work as a practical guide:

1. **One URL per language.** Google recommends a different URL for each version of a page, instead of switching the language with cookies or browser settings. For example, `/precios` and `/en/pricing`.
2. **Translate the whole page.** Google uses the visible text of the page to work out its language, and it does not use the `lang` attribute. It also says translating only the menu and buttons can make for a bad experience.
3. **Do not redirect by language alone.** Google asks you not to send people to another version by guessing their language. Put a visible link to switch languages.
4. **Link both versions with hreflang.** That tells Google which version belongs to each language so it shows the right one.

This blog works that way: every article is in Spanish and English, each at its own URL, and each version points to the other with hreflang.

Also check that the translation makes sense to the person reading it: prices in the currency you charge in, real shipping terms and examples from that market.

## How do I track the same questions in both languages?

1. **Pick what to measure in each language.** If you sell in both, split your 20 questions, for example 10 in Spanish and 10 in English, with the same intent in each pair: "¿cuánto cuesta [tu servicio] en Bogotá?" and "how much does [your service] cost in Bogotá?".
2. **Keep them written the way your customer writes them.** Each question is asked exactly as written. When your Peaje agent picks them, it writes them in your customers' language, and if your market is Latin America it mixes Spanish and English.
3. **Change them whenever you want.** Add or remove questions from your dashboard or by asking your agent, up to 20 at a time.
4. **Compare every week.** With Pro, your questions are measured once a week in ChatGPT and Claude. Look at which language cites you and who gets cited instead in the other one.
5. **Measure each translation as a new page.** When you publish the English version of a page, Peaje measures it again at 2 and 6 weeks, like any other.
